# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Who is the exact target user?

**Technical co-founders and engineering leads at seed-to-Series-A startups (5–50 people) who manage their team's AI tool stack.** Not "startups" generically — a specific person: the CTO or VP Eng who approves the monthly Cursor/ChatGPT/Claude subscriptions and sees the aggregate cost on their credit card statement. They're technical enough to evaluate our recommendations but busy enough to appreciate a 60-second audit over a manual spreadsheet exercise.

## Where do they hang out online?

1. **Hacker News** — Primary distribution channel. A Show HN post with a provocative title ("We audited 200 startups' AI tool spend. The median team wastes $4,200/year.") can drive 5,000–15,000 visits in 48 hours if it hits the front page. This audience is skeptical, technically literate, and exactly our buyer.

2. **Twitter/X (tech/founder community)** — Target accounts: founders who tweet about their stack, VC partners who share cost optimization advice, and DevTools influencers. A viral tweet thread ("Your AI tool stack is bleeding money. Here's the math.") with a link to the audit tool can generate 50–200 qualified visits per day with zero ad spend.

3. **Indie Hackers + r/startups** — Secondary channels for organic seeding. Post in weekly "Share what you're working on" threads with a genuine ask for feedback.

4. **Engineering Slack communities** — LaunchDark, Rands Leadership, DevOps Chat. Share the tool as a resource, not a pitch.

## How to get the first 100 users in 30 days with $0 budget

**Week 1:** Soft launch. Share with 10 founder friends, collect feedback, fix UX issues. Get 3 real testimonials with savings numbers.

**Week 2:** Show HN post. This is the single highest-leverage action. Craft the post title to lead with the insight ("The median startup wastes $4,200/year on AI tools"), not the product ("We built a tool to..."). Include a direct link to the live audit.

**Week 3:** Twitter thread campaign. Write 3 threads: (1) "I audited my own team's AI spend. Here's what I found." (2) Pricing comparison breakdown (Cursor vs Copilot vs Windsurf). (3) "The hidden costs of AI team plans." Each thread ends with a link to the audit tool.

**Week 4:** Cold outreach to 50 CTOs on LinkedIn with personalized messages referencing their team's likely tool stack (visible from job posts mentioning Cursor/Copilot). Offer to run a free audit call.

## The unfair distribution channel

**Credex's existing customer base.** If Credex already sells infrastructure credits, every sales conversation is a natural entry point for the audit tool. "Before we quote you, let's see where you're overspending." The tool becomes a pre-sale qualification mechanism — it identifies the highest-value prospects (>$500/mo savings) and warms them up before a human touches the deal.
